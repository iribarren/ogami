## Setting

A pre-modern hard country in late autumn. Frost, mud, bare trees, a low grey sky and light that is already shortening. Fords, high moor, byres, a road, and at the end a shrine that is seen only as a gate. The time of year follows the flow's clock, Winter and first snow (voice pack, Lexicon EN).

## Palette and materials

Ink drawing with the weight of a woodcut and a cold, thin wash. Peat brown, frost grey and the tallow-yellow of a single lantern or fire, which is the only warm light. Every picture reads in grayscale. The things in it are plain and village-made: rope, wooden poles, wool, leather, a clay or iron pot. This follows the register, with its "short words, concrete nouns" and village speech, not a court's (Register).

## Motifs

- The litter: two poles, rope, a shrouded shape
- Bearers' feet, boots, rag wrappings, a hand on a pole
- A ford, a frayed rope on a willow, a byre wall, a dying fire
- The kit, laid out: rope, lantern, staff, waterskin, bandages, knife, sling
- An empty shrine gate in the first snow

## Mood

Weary, devout and unheroic, the tone The Long Carry promises (Register). Prayer is part of the work, as weather is, and is never staged. Death is present and plain, because the old bearer has seen too much of it to be delicate.

## Composition

Figures are small, seen from behind or at a distance, never their faces. The weather and the ground take most of the frame. What is carried is always a shrouded shape on a litter, never uncovered.

## Taboos

- No heroics: nobody wins against the weather, and no pose of courage or triumph (Taboos: "No heroics").
- No church iconography, crosses, haloes or named doctrine (Taboos: "No theology").
- Nothing modern: no machines, plastics, tidy uniforms or paved roads (Taboos: "No modern words").
- No humour or winking, so no caricature or comic detail (Taboos: "No humour").
- Nothing from another game or setting: no borrowed gods, symbols or costumes (Taboos: "Nothing taken from another game").
- The saint is never shown as a corpse or a face, and the bearers are unnamed and faceless; the carried body is always a shrouded shape on a litter (Persona; Lexicon EN, "the corpse" is avoided).
- No lettering, numbers, symbols, faces or gore (shared rules, README).
