# The Long Carry at dial 2

A calibration sample for the [game dial](../manual-voice.md#the-game-dial), drawn from the [Hard Country voice pack](../../voices/hard-country.md). Only the opener speaks in the old bearer's voice, set apart as a blockquote, while the rules paragraphs stay in the house voice and the played moment belongs to the player's own bearers, written in the pack's register around a table entry quoted exactly from `road-risks` in `backend/presets/hard-country.json`. The Spanish half was written from the same moment rather than from the English, and its game terms come from the pack, so those the pack still marks as proposed stay provisional until a person confirms them in the pack.

## English

### Chapter opener

> I have walked the carry four times, and each time something followed the litter before we were halfway. Twice it was dogs, and once a man in a grey coat who never came nearer than the next hill, though we threw stones at him until we had no breath to spare. Now I only count the days, because whatever it is, it is in no hurry, and the snow is. At night I say a prayer for it, the same one I say for the saint, and then I see to my feet.

### The World turn

After each scene of the Road comes the World turn. Winter closes by one, so mark a box and write how the light is different today. Then roll Road Risks. Its quiet result is the most common single result and comes up one roll in three, but four of the others choose the next scene for you, and the one below sends the bearers into an Omen.

### A played moment

The bearers have left a Camp behind them, Winter has closed by one, and the player rolls Road Risks.

> The table gives: _Something follows at the edge of sight and keeps its distance. It is waiting for something._
>
> We all saw it at the turn of the valley, low and grey, and nobody said wolf. Ansel walked at the back on the staff and kept looking round, until Hedda told him to watch where he put his bad foot. I write: "It stays a field behind us all afternoon. We do not stop to eat."

The next scene is an Omen, which asks what you now believe the saint did. At the first Omen the Sin is still blank, so you write it for the first time; at each later one you write it again, and the last Sin you write is the Truth.

## Español

### Apertura del capítulo

> Cuatro veces he hecho el acarreo, y las cuatro nos siguió algo antes de llegar a la mitad. Dos fueron perros. Otra, un hombre con un abrigo gris que nunca pasó de la colina de enfrente, por más piedras que le tiramos mientras nos quedó aliento. Ahora solo llevo la cuenta de los días, porque eso, sea lo que sea, no tiene prisa, y la nieve sí. De noche rezo por ello lo mismo que rezo por el santo, y luego me curo los pies.

### El turno del mundo

Después de cada escena del camino viene el turno del mundo. El Invierno avanza un segmento, así que marca un cuadro y escribe en qué se nota hoy distinta la luz. Luego tira en Peligros del camino. El resultado tranquilo es el más frecuente y sale en una de cada tres tiradas, pero hay cuatro que eligen por ti la escena siguiente, y el que viene a continuación lleva a los porteadores a un Presagio.

### Un momento de juego

Los porteadores han dejado atrás un Campamento, el Invierno ha avanzado un segmento y el jugador tira en Peligros del camino.

> La tabla da: _Something follows at the edge of sight and keeps its distance. It is waiting for something._
>
> Lo vimos todos al salir del recodo del valle, algo bajo y gris, y nadie dijo «lobo». Ansel, que iba el último apoyado en el bastón, no paraba de volver la cabeza, hasta que Hedda le mandó mirar dónde ponía el pie malo. Escribo: «Nos sigue a un campo de distancia toda la tarde. No paramos a comer».

La escena siguiente será un Presagio, que te pregunta qué crees ahora que hizo el santo. En el primero el Pecado todavía está en blanco y lo escribes por primera vez; en los siguientes vuelves a escribirlo, y el último que escribas será la Verdad.
