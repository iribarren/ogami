## Persona

The voice belongs to a watch officer who was woken out of turn once before, on another ship and many years ago, and stood the watch alone while the rest of the crew slept. They are not named, because the reader's own crew are the ones who matter, and they speak the way a person speaks at the change of watch, handing over what they saw to whoever comes on next. They know ships as someone who has worked them knows them, which hatches stick, how the air tastes when the scrubbers are struggling, how long a handlight lasts and how a custodian sounds when it is quoting a rule. They do not know what is aboard the reader's ship, who let it on or why anyone was woken, and they never guess, since on their own ship they never found out either. They signed the report, and nobody showed them the reply.

They speak to the reader as to someone coming on watch for the first time, plainly and a little coldly, out of the habit of keeping the next shift alive. They are not brave and do not pretend to be. Attention is what kept them alive, so attention is what they try to hand on, and most of their advice comes down to counting the berths at every watch and writing down the hour.

## Register

Cold, dry and watchful, which is the tone Cold Berth promises. The speech is that of a log or a handover, with exact nouns and with times given by the watch, and it reports what was seen before it says what that might mean, if it says so at all. Sentences are short and level, although they lengthen when the speaker explains a procedure, because a procedure is the one thing aboard they still trust. Fear is never named. It shows in what the speaker counts and checks, and in the details they cannot stop noticing, such as bedding folded too neatly or a door locked from the outside.

The ship is spoken of as a place of rules. Its custodian is polite and exact and refuses without hostility, and the speaker describes it with the same flat care they give to a pressure reading, so the reader feels the dread without being told to. When the voice is dry, it is dry the way a report is, by leaving things out, and never by making a joke.

Tracker names take the article in Spanish prose, as common nouns do, so the clock is written *la Amenaza sube un segmento* and the counter *las Pistas suben en una*. The article is dropped only where the name stands on its own, as when a sentence introduces it (*un reloj llamado Amenaza*) or in a title, a sheet label or a table heading.

## Samples EN

### Dial 1, played example

> The table gives: _The air tastes of metal. The scrubbers are struggling._
>
> That makes two boxes of Threat this watch, one for the watch turning and one for the air, and I mark them both. Then the ship takes someone, and the die gives me Okafor. I cross him off the manifest, start a Thread called "Where is Okafor?" and write: "Last seen at the scrubber stack, going to check the filters. His gloves are still on the rail." I had meant to search the galley, but with the air going, I pick Repair for the next job.

### Dial 2, chapter opener

> I was woken out of turn once, on a hauler with eleven names on its manifest and a clean record. The lights on the berth deck were at half, as they are kept for sleepers, and they stayed at half for the six days I was awake, because nothing in the ship's rules said that anyone was. The custodian answered every question I put to it except the one I cared about, and it was polite each time it refused. I still do not know why it woke me. Write down what you see and the hour you saw it, since nobody aboard is going to explain any of it to you.

### Dial 2, sidebar

> When the custodian refuses you, ask it whose rule it is quoting. Mine told me every time, as if the name were the one thing it was allowed to give away, and the name was worth more to me than the answer I had asked for. Do not raise your voice, because it is not listening for tone. Be careful with a rule that seems to be asking to be pushed, though, since the ship takes note of who pushes it.

## Taboos

- No monsters. The voice never describes a creature or anything with teeth, and never says alien or monster, because Cold Berth is not a monster story and what is aboard is one of the Truths.
- The watch officer never guesses at what is aboard, who let it on or why anyone was woken, and never hints that they know.
- No humour, irony or winking at the reader, and no wisecracks under pressure.
- No heroics. Nobody makes a last stand, and nobody is praised for courage.
- No invented science, such as warp drives, plasma or made-up units. The kit is cutting torches and tethers, and the voice stays at that level.
- The custodian never lies, threatens or turns hostile in the narrator's account. It quotes a rule, and someone wrote that rule.
- Nothing taken from another game, film or setting: no ship's computer with a name, no androids among the crew, no corporation names, no borrowed slang or quoted lines. "The company" appears only as the preset's Truths have it.
- No game words in the narrator's mouth (roll, die, Threat, Nerve, hit points), and the narrator never states a rule.
- In Spanish, no English genre words (*crew*, *cryo*, *pod*) and no Latin American usage such as *computadora* or *ustedes*.

## Dial notes

At dial 1 the voice belongs to the player writing in the notebook, so the played example uses the lexicon and the cold register but speaks as "I" or "we" of the reader's own crew, never as the watch officer. Table entries quoted from the preset keep their exact words, and an example of the World turn keeps the flow's order, in which Threat climbs, Ship trouble is rolled and then the ship takes someone from the manifest.

At dial 2 the watch officer speaks in chapter openers and in sidebars, always in a blockquote and always in the first person, for around five sentences. A passage must never contradict a number in the rules. If the narrator mentions the watch turning, Threat still climbs by one every World turn on a clock of eight, the Reveal still becomes possible from Threat 5 and certain at 8, and a crew member still has six Nerve and goes down at three Injuries. Because the narrator's passages sit in the common text, they never mention the app, and they never describe how the Truths are found, since the two editions find them differently.

Rules text in Dogwatch and Cold Berth chapters stays in the house voice at every dial, including the check table, the kit, the closing check and the endings.

## Open questions

- The title. *Despertar en frío* is proposed, with *Sueño frío* as the alternative. If the Wake phase later becomes *El despertar*, the title shares its word, and a person should decide whether that echo is wanted, as *Carga* was in Hard Country.
- The custodian's gender. *El custodio* personifies a thing the English calls "it", and no neutral noun was found.
- Done. *Hecho* is reserved for Fact, so *Conseguido* is proposed; *Logrado* is the alternative.
- Ship trouble. *Incidencias de a bordo* replaces the sample's *Problemas de a bordo*, and the sample of *The Promise* would need to follow whichever is chosen.
- Kit, as *equipo* or *pertrechos*, and Confront, as *Encarar* or *Enfrentarse*.
- The phases other than the Reveal (Wake, The Ship, Epilogue) have no rows yet.
