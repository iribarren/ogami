# Cards and Dice as Oracles

You do not need a printed table to ask the world a question. A deck of playing cards and a handful of dice are enough, and both have things tables are bad at: cards run out, and dice can be read in more than one way.

This chapter is about those two tools, used as oracles in their own right. Tables are still the right tool when you want a specific line. Cards and dice are for the moments when you want something looser, or when you do not have the table with you.

## A deck of cards

A standard deck of 52 gives you four suits and thirteen ranks. Decide once what they mean and write it on a card.

**Draw to answer.** Shuffle and draw one. Red is yes and black is no. The rank gives the size of it: a low card is a small answer and a face card is a big one. An ace is always the extreme, either way. That gives you a likelihood oracle with a built-in "how much".

**Draw to ask what.** Give each suit a kind of thing: hearts for people, spades for danger, clubs for places, diamonds for things you can carry. A draw tells you which kind of thing is about to turn up. The rank tells you how close it is: a two is far off, a king is in the room.

**Draw to count.** Use the rank as a number when you need one, an ace being one and a face card ten. The number of guards, the days of a journey, the weight of a purse.

### Drawing and reshuffling

A deck is not a die. It has a memory, and that is why it is useful.

- **Draw without putting back.** Each card you turn is gone. After twenty draws the deck has told you what it will no longer say. An empty suit is an answer.
- **Reshuffle at a moment, not by habit.** Reshuffle when the story turns: a new session, a new Phase, a change of place. Then the deck tells you about the beginning again.
- **Keep a discard pile.** Cards you have used stay face up beside the deck. You can see what has been spent.
- **Seed a card.** Put a card you want, say a king of spades, in the bottom third and shuffle the rest on top. It will come, but not yet.

The last is the paper deck of Part I: the road deck of The Long Carry seeds the Shrine Gate near the bottom, so the end comes late, but not at the same place twice.

## Handfuls of dice

A few dice thrown at once give you more than one number, and the spread is the answer.

- **Odds and evens.** Throw three dice. Count the evens. Three is a clean yes and none is a clean no. One or two is a yes, but, or a no, but, as you like.
- **Highest and lowest.** Throw several and take the highest and the lowest. The highest is how good it gets, the lowest is how bad. The gap between them is how much is at stake.
- **Pairs.** If two dice match, something has an echo. Decide what it means before you play: a repeat, a witness, a coincidence.
- **A pool for a count.** Throw a handful and count the dice that show 5 or 6. That is the number of things going wrong, or right.

Choose your rule before you throw and write it down. A rule you make up after the dice have landed is a rule that agrees with what you wanted.

## Choosing between them

Reach for the tool that fits the question.

| You want                       | Use                                |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| A yes or no with a size        | A single card, red or black        |
| A specific line of prose       | A table and one die                |
| Something that gets used up    | A deck, drawn without putting back |
| A spread: how good and how bad | A handful of dice                  |

Mixing them is fine. Draw a card for the kind of thing, then roll a die on the table for that kind. Do not mix two systems in one question, or you will not remember which result you were reading.

## On paper

Keep a half sheet headed **Cards and dice** with the rule for each tool in one line: what red and black mean, what each suit is, what the odds and evens do. Tape it inside the lid of the box.

Write what you drew in the margin beside the line it answered. A king of clubs in the margin is a reminder that the clubs have been used.

:::in-ogami
Ogami has no deck of playing cards. The **Dice** panel in Play rolls any dice expression and records the roll in the journal. It has a **Dice expression** field, such as 2d6+1, and quick rolls for d20, 2d6, 4d6kh3 and d%. The expression can keep the highest or lowest dice, with `kh`, `kl`, `dh` and `dl`, add and subtract dice and numbers, and use brackets.

A table does the work of a deck. A weighted table is a deck in which every entry has as many copies as its **Weight**. Every roll on it is fresh: nothing is removed, and there is no discard pile or reshuffle. Draw a physical deck at the table if you want a pile that runs out.
:::

## The Long Carry

The Long Carry's paper edition is the clearest use of a deck. Thirteen cards stand for the five Scene Types of the road: three Camps, two Fords, three Strangers, two Ruins and three Omens, plus the Shrine Gate shuffled into the bottom five.

A played draw. Four cards have gone: Camp, Omen, Stranger, Camp. The deck is on its fifth card.

> I turn the card: a Ford. I have drawn two Camps and no Fords. The deck had two Fords, so the river is coming whether I am ready or not. I put the Ford face up on the discard pile and begin the scene.

A deck remembers where you have been. A die would not.

## Cold Berth

Cold Berth has no deck. It lays out four job cards face up, and the player picks one, so its oracle is the choice. It does keep a pack of blank compartment cards, and they can be shuffled and drawn when you do not want to pick.

A played draw. Nine compartment cards, five named so far: the Galley, the Hold, the Bridge, Cryo and the Corridor. Okafor's job asks for a place, and the player wants it chosen for them.

> I shuffle the five named cards and draw one: Cryo. I turn it up, and set it aside. The next job cannot go there without a reason.

The deck has chosen where, and the used card says where not to.

## Try it

Set aside a deck and write one line for what red and black mean, one for what each suit means and one for when you reshuffle. Then play ten draws and keep the ones that made a scene.
