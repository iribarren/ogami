# Cards and Print

Played at a table, a flow is a pile of cards. Making the pile means deciding what goes on each card, how you draw from it, what happens to a card once it has been played and how to make the end of the story turn up late.

## Card anatomy

A flow on paper uses two kinds of card.

**A Scene Type card** is the one from Chapter 3.

- **Front:** the name, the purpose and the tips.
- **Back:** Setup, Play and Closing, each with its questions or its roll.

Print the name large, because the name is what you look for when you hunt through the pile for a card. Keep the back to what fits, since a step that takes a paragraph to explain does not belong on a card.

**A table card** is for a deck that stands in for an oracle table. Its front has a single line with the text of the entry and the name of the Scene Type that the entry starts. Each entry gets its own card, so a table of eight entries is eight cards, and an entry that carries a weight is printed once for each of its copies.

Since cards are cheap, give every Scene Type a card of its own, and keep everything else on a single sheet, the flow's rules card, which holds the clocks, the slots, the closing check and the endings. Each of those parts has its own chapter, with the Fact slots in Chapter 6, the clocks and the closing check in Chapter 7 and the endings grid in Chapter 9, and the rules card gathers them here on one sheet.

## Drawing

There are two ways to draw, and they suit different jobs.

- **In order.** You take the top card every time, and the deck is a sequence. Its author has already put the cards in order, and that order is part of the design.
- **At random.** You shuffle and draw, which makes the deck an oracle deck, where the order comes from the dice.

A third way sits between the two. You shuffle the deck, lay out a few cards face up and choose one of them. The result is a spread with a random menu, since the decision is yours but you can only make it among the cards the dice offered.

## Discard or reshuffle

Once a card has been played, it goes to one of two places.

**Back into the deck, reshuffled.** Every draw is then a fresh draw and the odds never change, which is what Chapter 5 assumed. Because the deck remembers nothing, three Fords in a row can happen.

**Onto a discard pile.** The deck gets shorter with each draw, and the odds shift as it does. When it runs out, you either stop or shuffle the discard pile to make a new deck. A deck you discard from is also a clock, since the shorter it gets, the nearer the end is.

Reshuffling suits a world that does not remember, such as a road, whereas discarding suits a story that should run out.

## Seeding a finale

When the deck is a clock, you can place the ending inside it. Before the first draw, take the finale card and shuffle it into the bottom part of the deck only, either the last third or the last five cards. The ending can then turn up late but never early, and because it does not turn up at a time you chose, it feels found.

To make the story the length you want, count the cards and place the finale to match. A deck of fifteen with the finale in the bottom five ends between the eleventh and the fifteenth draw.

## On paper

The Long Carry plays from the road deck, which holds thirteen copies of five cards, with the Shrine Gate card shuffled into the bottom of the pile. Discard each card as you play it. **The Road** ends when the Shrine Gate card turns up or when the closing check on Winter ends it, whichever comes first.

Cold Berth needs no deck for its finale, because the **Reveal** comes from Threat, a clock you can see, and so a round of **The Ship** never has to hide an ending in a pile. Its cards are the four job cards, laid face up, and one card for each compartment you named in **Session Zero**.

Make the cards yourself. Each Scene Type needs an index card, or a page of your notebook, and each deck needs the right number of copies.

:::in-ogami
Ogami has no deck. A draw is a roll on the oracle table that the Phase names, so the road deck is The road deck table, and each copy is a point of its **Weight**. Every roll is a fresh one: there is no discard pile and nothing is removed from the table.

The paper deck's seeded finale has no counterpart. The Shrine Gate is not a card. The Road ends through two steps in its **Scene closing**: a **condition** on Winter, then a **roll** of a d6, and each has a band with an `endPhase` effect. The Shrine Gate is the next Phase, played in sequence.

In Play, the scene pick shows the Scene Type's purpose and tips on the card it offers.
:::

## The Long Carry

On paper The Long Carry plays out of a pile that is also a clock, with thirteen road cards and the Shrine Gate card somewhere among the bottom five. Every card you play brings you a step nearer the gate, although you cannot say which step will be the last.

In this draw from the paper deck, nine cards are already on the discard pile and five remain, with the Shrine Gate card among them.

> I turn the top card: _Omen: the road says something about the saint_. I play it.
>
> Next card: _Ruin: what the road left behind_. Then the Camp. Then the Shrine Gate.

The gate arrived late, on a day that you did not choose and the pile did.

## Cold Berth

**The Ship** needs no deck, but its compartments make a small one, since each compartment has a card that carries its name and a line about it. In a round you either lay the cards out face up and take one, or shuffle them and draw. Laying them out makes the place a choice, while shuffling them gives you the feeling of a ship that is out of your hands.

The crew has named three compartments, so the pile holds three cards, the Wake Bay, the Drive Spine and the Cold Hold.

> I shuffle them, because I do not trust my own choice.
>
> I draw the Cold Hold. I had wanted the Drive Spine, which is the loud one. The Cold Hold is where the fourth sleeper is, and I have been avoiding it.

You can let the pile choose on purpose, as a way to send yourself where you were not going to.

## Try it

Count the Scene Types in your flow. Then decide, for each Phase, whether its cards are drawn in order, at random or from a spread, and whether a played card is reshuffled or discarded. If you want the ending to turn up late, decide how many cards from the bottom you will seed it.
