# Journaling and Lists

Solo play has one great weakness. When you play with other people, they remember for you, whereas when you play alone, whatever you do not write down is gone by next week.

Two things make up for it. **Lists** keep the cast, on pages you turn to the way you would turn to the index of a book, and a **journal** keeps the story, much as a travel diary keeps a journey, day by day and in the order it happened.

## The journal

::image{file="house-journal.webp" style="house" size="half" alt="An open notebook with faint pencil lines, one of them struck through." subject="An open notebook with a few faint pencil lines of handwriting too small to read, one line struck through, a pencil beside it."}

The journal is a log of what happened, kept in order. It does not have to be good writing, since its job is to let you pick up the game after a month and know where you are.

For each scene write:

- **A heading.** The session, the scene and the Scene Type, as in "Session 2, scene 5, Ford."
- **What you rolled.** The question, the odds and the answer, and for a table, the table and the line.
- **What happened.** Two to five sentences in plain past tense.
- **What changed.** A Tracker that moved, a Thread opened, a name added to a list.

Write while you play rather than afterwards, because a scene written up the next morning has already lost its small surprises.

### What to write

Write what you will need and what you will enjoy. That means the decisions, with what the character chose and what they gave up to choose it, and the answers you did not expect, giving a full line to any "no, but" you would not have written yourself. Write a name down the first time it turns up, with one detail and where it came from. When someone says they will do something, write that down as well, because it becomes a Thread.

### What to let go

You cannot write everything, and you should not try. Let the ordinary go, and if the bearers made camp and nothing happened, "Camp. Quiet." is the entry. When a check moved neither a Tracker nor the scene, you do not need its dice, and when you wrote a line and then crossed it out, leave it crossed out. Anything you can look up needs only its number, because the table already has the line.

When you are not sure, ask whether you would miss it three sessions from now, and if you would not, let it go.

## Lists

A **list** is a page of your notebook that keeps one kind of thing, and a solo game wants four of them.

| List    | Keeps                       | You add to it when                                  |
| ------- | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Threads | Open questions              | A line asks one, someone gives their word           |
| NPCs    | People, with what they want | A table gives someone, a scene needs a name         |
| Places  | Locations, with a detail    | A scene happens somewhere you may go back to        |
| Facts   | What is now true            | You decide something the story will have to respect |

Memory, in Part I, says what each list is for. To keep them, give each entry one line, a name and a clause, and when an entry needs a paragraph, write it as the two entries it really is. Close entries instead of erasing them, striking through a Thread when it ends or an NPC when they leave, because a struck name is still a name you can remember.

At the end of each session, read each list once and prune it on purpose, closing what cannot matter any more.

When you need a person who is already in the story, number the open NPCs and roll on the list, which is the cheapest way to make the world feel small.

Weight your Threads as well, so that the page tells you which questions matter most. Mark the ones that do, and when you want a Thread to come back, roll on those first.

## On paper

Use one notebook, with the journal at the front and the four lists at the back, each on a page of its own. Leave room in the lists for crossing things out, and date every entry.

At the end of a session, write the three lines you will be glad of on the day you pick the notebook up again:

1. Where we are.
2. What is open.
3. What I want to see next.

:::in-ogami
In Play, the **Journal** records every roll and answer as it happens: dice rolls, oracle answers and table results. It is grouped by session and then by scene. To write something of your own, use **New note** and **Write note**. **Attach results** on the note composer lets you attach up to 20 earlier results to it.

The lists are Play panels: **Facts**, **Threads**, **Places** and **NPCs**. Each has an add button (**Add Thread**, **Add Place**, **Add NPC**, **Add fact**), and its entries can be edited, given a **Weight**, and closed or reopened with **Close** and **Reopen**. An NPC also has an **Agenda**.

A flow's steps can open Threads and NPCs for you, with effects such as `createThread` and `createNpc`, so they need not be typed twice.
:::

## The Long Carry

The Long Carry produces a lot of small material, such as a Stranger, a Ruin or an Omen, and its journal is mostly the road, one line each, with the Sin rewritten every time an Omen changes it.

An entry for an Omen scene might read like this.

> Session 1, scene 4. Omen. Omens: _Dogs that will not cross the shadow of the litter_. Sin: now "The saint is owed. The dogs know who by." Winter 5, Burden 1.

Keep the Threads page short. Put the question of the whole game on its first line, "Will we reach the shrine before snow?", and let it stand until the **Epilogue**, adding another Thread only when a Stranger gives their word.

## Cold Berth

Cold Berth opens Threads quickly, because its trouble is people. The ship takes a crew member each round, and a Thread opens for each of them, as "Where is Ines?" did for Ines.

One round of **The Ship**, written up, might look like this.

> Session 2, scene 6. Investigate, the Galley. Treason: a falsified cryo log. Suspect: Pell. Clues 3. Thread: Who falsified the cryo log? Ines lost. Threat 5.

Two of the notebook's lists matter in play. The manifest of NPCs tagged crew is the one the ship takes from, and the list of Places tagged compartment is the one every job asks for. The crew list is meant to shrink as the loop goes on.

## Try it

Write a journal entry of five lines for the last scene you played, then cross out every line you would not miss three sessions from now. What is left is the entry you should have written.
