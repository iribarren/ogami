# Licensing and Credits

A GameSystem you publish is read by other people. This page is about what you say about it: who made it, what they may do with it, and what you owe to those who inspired it.

## Credits

A GameSystem carries optional **Credits**: a notice, a license, a link and a badge. They are filled in on the Overview of the GameSystem, along with the name, description and theme. Play shows them where a GameSystem is chosen and on the campaign page.

| Field   | What goes in it                                             |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Notice  | A sentence for the reader: who made this, and on what terms |
| License | The license by name (optional)                              |
| Link    | An `https` address (optional), for example the license text |
| Badge   | A publisher's logo from a closed list (optional)            |

A license, a link or a badge need a notice to go with them. A notice can stand alone.

Both GameSystems in this book use the same shape. The notice for Hard Country reads: "Hard Country is an original game written for the Ogami manual, with its flow The Long Carry. It is released under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International license." Its license field says CC BY-SA 4.0, and its link goes to the license text. Dogwatch's notice is the same, with its own name and its flow Cold Berth. Neither uses a badge.

Credits belong to a release. Publish a release and the Credits go with it, so a campaign pinned to version 2 shows the notice of version 2.

## Choosing a license

Pick a license for your GameSystem before you share it, not after.

- **Rules you wrote entirely yourself** are yours to license. A Creative Commons license is a good fit for tabletop text: CC BY asks only for credit, and CC BY-SA also asks that changes are shared on the same terms.
- **Rules built on someone else's** come with that license's terms. If you adapt a game released under a license, read what it asks about credit and sharing, and use the notice to say what you owe. This book gives no legal advice.
- **A publisher's game** is not yours to publish without permission. A fan GameSystem put the publisher's disclaimer in the notice, and the badge is for the publisher logos Studio knows about. Do not publish the text of a rulebook you do not have rights to.

Hard Country and Dogwatch were written from scratch for this book, so there was nothing to inherit. The whole book, both GameSystems included, is CC BY-SA 4.0, kept as a decision of the book and not forced by any source.

## "Inspired by" lists

Studio has no "Inspired by" field. A Credits notice is a free sentence, and the manual's own credits page keeps the lists. If you want yours in the app, put it in the notice and keep it short, or in a page next to the GameSystem.

An "Inspired by" list is about ideas, not text. Hard Country's flow was inspired by the translation of a saint's body, a novel about a family's journey with a coffin, and a film about a promise to bury a friend. Cold Berth was inspired by three films about people woken or stranded aboard ships. The book takes from them a situation and a mood. It takes no text, no names, no characters and no plot.

Say what you leave out. The book's credits say Cold Berth avoids the creature life cycle, the android-traitor reveal and the plot sequence of one of its three films. A list that names the line you did not cross is a better list.

## Writing your own words

The rule for everything you publish is simple: write it yourself.

- Do not copy rules text, even a sentence, and then change a few words. If you cannot say it without the original open, you have not understood it.
- Take a mechanism, not its wording. "Roll under a trait" is an idea. A paragraph describing it is somebody's text. Hard Country uses a roll-under d20 Check and says so in its own sentence.
- Invent your names. Names of places, creatures and people from another game belong to that game.
- Quote only to credit, and sparingly.

When in doubt, write the rule down in plain words, close the source, and read it back as a stranger would.

## Try it

Write the notice for your GameSystem in two sentences: who made it and on what terms. Then write three lines of "Inspired by", each with the idea you took and one thing you left out.
