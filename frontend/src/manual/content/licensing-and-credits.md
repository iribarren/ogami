# Licensing and Credits

A GameSystem you publish is read by other people, and this chapter is about what you tell them alongside the rules, which is who made the game, what they may do with it and what you owe to those who inspired it.

## Credits

A printed rulebook says who made it on its credits page, and a GameSystem carries the same kind of statement in its optional **Credits**, made of a notice, a license, a link and a badge. You fill them in on the Overview of the GameSystem, along with its name, description and theme. Play shows them where a GameSystem is chosen and on the campaign page.

| Field   | What goes in it                                             |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Notice  | A sentence for the reader: who made this, and on what terms |
| License | The license by name (optional)                              |
| Link    | An `https` address (optional), for example the license text |
| Badge   | A publisher's logo from a closed list (optional)            |

A license, a link or a badge needs a notice to go with it, whereas a notice can stand alone.

Both GameSystems in this book use the same shape. The notice for Hard Country reads "Hard Country is an original game written for the Ogami manual, with its flow The Long Carry. It is released under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International license." Its license field says CC BY-SA 4.0, and its link goes to the license text. Dogwatch's notice is the same, with its own name and its flow Cold Berth, and neither GameSystem uses a badge.

Credits belong to a release. When you publish a release the Credits go with it, so a campaign pinned to version 2 shows the notice of version 2.

## Choosing a license

Pick a license for your GameSystem before you share it, not after. Which license you can pick depends on where the rules came from.

Rules you wrote entirely yourself are yours to license. A Creative Commons license is a good fit for tabletop text in either of two forms, which differ in what they ask of anyone who reuses it. CC BY asks only for credit, and CC BY-SA also asks that changes are shared on the same terms.

Rules built on someone else's come with the terms of the license those rules were released under. If you adapt a game released under a license, read what it asks about credit and sharing, and use the notice to say what you owe. This book gives no legal advice.

A publisher's game is not yours to publish without permission. A fan GameSystem puts the publisher's disclaimer in the notice, and the badge is there for the publisher logos that Studio knows about. Do not publish the text of a rulebook you do not have the rights to.

Hard Country and Dogwatch were written from scratch for this book, so there was nothing to inherit. The whole book, both GameSystems included, is CC BY-SA 4.0, and that license is a decision of the book rather than something any source forced on it.

## "Inspired by" lists

Studio has no "Inspired by" field. A Credits notice is a free sentence, and the manual keeps its own lists on its credits page. If you want yours in the app, put it in the notice and keep it short, or put it in a page next to the GameSystem.

An "Inspired by" list is about ideas rather than text. Hard Country's flow was inspired by the translation of a saint's body, a novel about a family's journey with a coffin and a film about a promise to bury a friend. Cold Berth was inspired by three films about people woken or stranded aboard ships. From these the book takes a situation and a mood, and it takes no text, no names, no characters and no plot.

Say what you leave out, too, because a list that names the line you did not cross is a better list. The book's credits do this for Cold Berth, saying that it avoids the creature life cycle, the android-traitor reveal and the plot sequence of one of its three films.

## Writing your own words

Everything you publish should be written by you, in your own words. Do not copy rules text, even a single sentence, and then change a few words, because if you cannot say it without the original open, you have not understood it.

Take a mechanism and leave its wording behind. "Roll under a trait" is an idea, whereas a paragraph describing it is somebody's text. Hard Country uses a roll-under d20 Check and says so in a sentence of its own.

Invent your names, since the names of places, creatures and people from another game belong to that game. Quote only to give credit, and do it sparingly.

When in doubt, write the rule down in plain words, close the source and read your version back as a stranger would.

## Try it

Write the notice for your GameSystem in two sentences, saying who made it and on what terms. Then write three lines of "Inspired by", each with the idea you took and one thing you left out.
