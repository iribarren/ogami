# Memory

A game master remembers for the table. Who the stranger was, what the saint is said to have done, which door was locked: the game master holds it all, and the players take it for granted.

When you play alone, nobody holds it. Two sessions later you will not remember that the widow wanted her husband's boots taken to the shrine, and if you do not remember it, the world did not remember it either. A solo game needs something that remembers for you. A flow gives you five places to keep what matters.

## Five places

**Facts.** A Fact is a statement about the world that you now hold to be true: "The saint was a ferryman." Some Facts are written in a **slot**, a labelled blank that the flow gives you. The Long Carry has three: the Saint, the Sin and the Vow. A slot can be filled again, and what you write last replaces what you wrote before. That is how a belief changes. Other Facts are free, written when you want them.

**Threads.** A Thread is a question the story has opened and not yet answered: "Where is Ines?", "Who falsified the cryo log?". It is open until you answer it, and then you close it. Threads are how the flow remembers what it still owes you.

**NPCs.** An NPC is a person who is not one of the characters you play. A name is enough, and a line about what they want. An NPC is open while they are part of the story, and closed when they are gone.

**Places.** A Place is somewhere the story has been or may go: a shrine, a hold, a compartment of a ship. A Place can be open or closed too.

**Tags.** A tag is a word you attach to an NPC, a Thread or a Place so the flow can find it later. "Crew" on a person means the ship may take them. "Compartment" on a Place means the crew may go there. A tag is how a list becomes a deck: you can ask for a random crew member, or a compartment to choose from.

An NPC, a Thread or a Place can carry a **weight** from one to three. A weight of three means it matters more, and it comes up more often when something is drawn from the list.

## What deserves writing down

Not everything. A notebook that records every detail is a notebook you stop reading. Write down a thing when one of these is true.

- **You will want to find it again.** A name, a place, a promise.
- **It changes something.** A suspicion that moves a clock, a belief that moves the ending.
- **The flow will ask for it.** A pick step needs a list to pick from. If Session Zero did not make it, the Phase will find an empty page.

If none is true, it was colour. Enjoy it, and let it go.

Keep each entry to a line. If you need a paragraph, you have two entries.

## On paper

Use four lists in one notebook, one page each.

| List    | What you write                              | Columns                                |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Facts   | The slots at the top, then free Facts below | The label, then the current text       |
| Threads | One line per open question                  | The line, a weight, open or closed     |
| NPCs    | A name and what they want                   | The line, tags, weight, open or closed |
| Places  | A name and a word about it                  | The line, tags, weight, open or closed |

Write the slots on the first page before you play, even if they are blank. A blank line on page one is a promise to yourself: this will matter.

To close an entry, strike it through. Do not erase it. A crossed-out name is a person you can still remember. To change a slot, cross out the old line and write the new one beneath it. The last line is the truth.

When the flow asks you to pick someone, make the pick from the list. If you want it random, number the open entries that carry the tag and roll.

:::in-ogami
The notebook is the set of Play panels named **Facts**, **Threads**, **Places** and **NPCs**. The **Facts** panel lists the flow's slots, each with a **Fill** button (**Refill** once it holds a text), and the free Facts under them with **Add fact**. The other three have **Add Thread**, **Add Place** and **Add NPC**, and each entry can be edited, given a **Weight**, and closed or reopened with **Close** and **Reopen**. An NPC has an **Agenda** too, and can be promoted to a Character.

In Studio, the **Trackers & tags** page declares what a flow can use. **New tag** has a **Key**, a **Label**, a **Hint** and **Applies to**: Characters, NPCs, Threads or Places. **New fact slot** has the same fields and a **Type**: text, npc, thread or place. In a step, **Add effect** offers `createNpc`, `createThread`, `createPlace`, `closeThread` and `fillFact`, and the first three take **Tags**.

A step's text can read a slot as `{fact:sin}`, using the key of the slot.
:::

## The Long Carry

The Long Carry remembers three things in slots, and everything else it lets you keep as you like.

| Slot      | What it holds                             |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| The saint | Whose body you carry                      |
| The Sin   | What the saint did, as you now believe it |
| The vow   | What you promised, and to whom            |

The saint and the vow are written once, in Session Zero. The Sin is the one that moves. Every Omen asks _What do you now believe the saint did?_ and the answer goes into the slot, replacing the one before. The slot's hint says it plainly: each Omen can change it.

The flow also makes people and places as it goes. Each Stranger becomes an NPC from the answer to _Who is on the road ahead?_ The shrine becomes a Place when you say where it stands. Nothing is tagged, because nothing is ever drawn from these lists; they are the notebook, nothing more.

A played Omen. The Sin so far reads: "A debt to a river, unpaid." The Omens table is rolled.

> The table gives: _Dogs that will not cross the shadow of the litter._
>
> I write: "Dogs go round us in a wide curve, as if the litter threw something on the ground. The saint did not owe the river. The river owes the saint, and has been waiting to collect." I cross out the old line and write the new one under it.

The previous Sin is struck but not erased. When the Shrine Gate asks what you carried, the flow reads the last line back to you.

## Cold Berth

Cold Berth remembers more, because it draws from its lists.

The three Truths are slots: _What is aboard_, _Who let it on_ and _Why we were woken_. They stay blank until the Reveal. Chapter 9 explains why.

The ship scene of Session Zero fills the other lists. Each compartment you name becomes a Place tagged **compartment**. Each person on the manifest becomes an NPC tagged **crew**. Those two tags are the reason the loop works: every job opens by asking for a compartment, and the world turn asks for a crew member.

The loop also opens Threads: "Where is Ines?" when someone goes missing, and "Who falsified the cryo log?" when a table asks it.

A played pick. The world turn asks the ship to take a crew member. The open crew on the manifest are Ines, Pell and Okafor. I number them in pairs and roll a d6: 1 or 2 is Ines, 3 or 4 is Pell, 5 or 6 is Okafor.

> The d6 gives a 2: Ines. The prompt reads: _Where were they last seen, and what was left behind?_ The flow opens a Thread, "Where is Ines?"
>
> I write: "Last seen at the manifest desk. Her cup is still warm." I cross Ines off the crew list, so the ship cannot take her twice, and add the Thread at the foot of the Threads page.

Closing the crew member is what keeps the ship honest. A person who is already gone cannot go again.

## Try it

List the five places for your flow. Write down which of them each Phase needs before it starts. For each tag, name the Phase that will ask for it, and the page where Session Zero makes the first one.
