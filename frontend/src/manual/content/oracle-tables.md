# Oracle Tables

A likelihood oracle answers a question. An **oracle table** answers "what?". What is on the road, who is at the door, what went wrong in the night. You roll, you read a line, and the line gives you something to build on.

Almost everything a flow rolls is a table. The Long Carry has Road Risks, Strangers, Omens and Ruins. Cold Berth has Ship trouble, Treason and three Truth tables. A table is a list of lines with a rule for choosing one.

## Three kinds

**Ranged.** Number the lines and roll the dice that cover them. A table of eight lines is a d8. A table of twelve is a d12, or two d6 added, if the lines run from 2 to 12. Every number must belong to a line, and no number may belong to two. Ranges are how you make some lines rarer: give the common one the numbers 1 to 4 and the strange one a single number.

**Weighted.** Some lines count more than once. Write a number beside each line for its copies. Road Risks has a quiet road with three copies and six other lines with one, nine in all. Roll a die that reaches nine, count through the copies in order, and stop on the line you land in. Weighted tables are easier to edit. Add a copy and the table grows by one; nothing needs renumbering.

**Nested.** A line can send you to another table. "A traveller falls into step beside you" can say: _roll Strangers_. You finish the first roll, follow the line and roll again. Use a nested table when one result needs a second answer to be usable, and do not nest more than twice. At three rolls the first has stopped mattering.

## Matching a die to a table

Pick the smallest die that covers the number of lines, and decide what to do with a roll above it.

| Lines | Die | If the roll is too high |
| ----- | --- | ----------------------- |
| 4     | d4  | none                    |
| 5     | d6  | roll again above 5      |
| 6     | d6  | none                    |
| 8     | d8  | none                    |
| 9     | d10 | roll again above 9      |
| 12    | d12 | none                    |

Rolling again is cleaner than bending the table. A d10 that lands on 10 is not a result. It is a second roll. Write the rule at the head of the table and do not think about it again.

Two dice give a bell. Two d6 reach 7 most often and 2 and 12 rarely. Use them when the middle lines should happen often and the ends should feel like events.

## Writing entries that prompt

An entry should hand you something to work with, not finish the work.

- **Specific, not general.** "A boy with a bell and no one to ring it for" is a person. "A child" is a category.
- **Open, not closed.** "A tinker who will not look at the litter" asks why. "A hostile tinker" answers.
- **A hook in each.** A name to give, a want to guess, a place to find. Aim for one thing the player has to decide.
- **Quiet lines.** Make one entry in three say that nothing happens. Without them nothing stands out.
- **Cost where it counts.** A table that moves the game can say so on the line. "A hard frost in the night" also moves Winter by one.

Make the lines about the same size. If one entry is a paragraph, it will take over every roll that lands near it.

## Testing a table

Before you play with a table, roll it ten times. Read the results aloud, one after the other, and ask three things.

- Did any line come up twice? That is fine. Did the same kind of line come up four times? The table is leaning.
- Did a result make you want to roll again? Find out why, and fix the line.
- Is there a line you hope you never roll? Keep it. It is the one that makes the others matter.

## On paper

Print or write a table as two columns: the number, then the line. Put the die at the top of the page. If the table has weights, write the numbers for each line as a range, so you never count copies at the table.

Note any effect beside the line, in brackets: **(Winter +1)**. Do it when you write the table, so you do not have to think of it again in play.

When you roll, read the line aloud and write one sentence of your own under it. The line is the prompt. Your sentence is the story.

:::in-ogami
A table is **Ranged (rolls dice)** or **Weighted (entries have a weight)**, chosen in **New table** on the **Oracles** page of a game system. A ranged table has a **Dice** field, such as 1d6, 2d6 or d%, and each entry has a **Min** and a **Max**. A weighted table has a **Weight** on each entry. In either, an entry can have a **Nested table**, a table of the same game system that is rolled next. Studio shows which numbers the ranges leave uncovered or cover twice.

In Play, the **Oracles** panel lists each table as a button, **Roll on** followed by its name. The result is recorded in the journal, and a nested table is followed to the end, all its steps shown.

Both shipped game systems use weighted tables only, and neither nests one.
:::

## The Long Carry

Road Risks is the table the Road rolls every turn. It has nine copies in seven lines, so a d10, with a roll of 10 rolled again. Three copies are the quiet road. The other six are one each: the risen ford, the hard frost, the bad foot, a traveller, smoke on a ridge, a thing that follows.

A played roll. The world turn rolls a d10: 7, which falls on the traveller.

> _A traveller falls into step beside you and starts to talk._ The line sends me to a Stranger scene, so I roll Strangers, a d8: 2.
>
> _A boy with a bell and no one to ring it for._ I write: "He keeps pace with the litter. When I ask who the bell is for, he says it is for whoever answers."

Road Risks does the first roll and the second one is a hand-made nest. The first table says _someone comes_ and the second says who.

## Cold Berth

Treason is rolled in an Investigate scene, at an optional step that asks whether someone is lying to you. It is a d8, and it mixes clues and threats: four give a Clue, two raise Threat, and two do nothing.

A played roll. The player doubts someone, and a d8 gives a 1.

> _A falsified cryo log: a sleeper's wake time was edited, and the edit carries a crew key. Name the suspect._
>
> The entry hands me a job, not a name. I write: "Pell. The edit is dated three nights before we woke, and Pell was the only one awake." Clues goes up by one, and I start a Thread: who falsified the cryo log?

The table gave the shape. The player gave the face.

## Try it

Write a table of eight entries for a place your flow visits. Make two of them quiet. Give each of the other six something a player has to decide.
