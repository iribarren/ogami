# Rules and Story

Parts I and II taught the story side of a game: a flow, its Scenes and the tools you roll at the table. This part is about the other half, the **rules**, and how they sit beside the story in Studio.

A **GameSystem** is the whole thing you author: a sheet, Checks, Gear, oracles, Trackers, Scene Types and flows. A **NarrativeFlow** is one guided way to play it. Studio keeps the two apart on purpose, and most design mistakes in a first GameSystem are a rule written into a story, or a story written into a rule.

## What belongs where

| The rules (the GameSystem)                        | The story (a NarrativeFlow)                            |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| The sheet: what a Character is made of            | The Phases and the order of play                       |
| Checks: how a die roll turns into an outcome      | Which Check a step offers, and what each outcome costs |
| Gear and Currency: what can be carried and bought | A shop step that sells some of it                      |
| Trackers, oracle tables, Fact slots, tags         | Which of them this flow reaches and reads              |
| Scene Types: reusable kinds of scene              | Which Scene Type a Phase picks, and in what order      |

The test is reuse. If a second story set in the same world would want the thing unchanged, it is a rule. If it only makes sense inside one story, it is the flow's.

Hard Country is the rules of a hard fantasy trek: three traits, a d20 Check, Wind and Wounds, a load of eight slots, pennies. The Long Carry is a story told with them. Nothing on the Hard Country sheet knows about the saint, the shrine or Winter. Winter is a Tracker, and the flow reaches it.

Dogwatch is the same split in space: four Stations, eight Checks, Nerve and Injuries, six slots of kit, chits. Cold Berth is the story. The sheet says nothing about a berth or a custodian.

## One GameSystem, many flows

A GameSystem release holds zero or more flows, and every flow in it shares the same sheet, Checks and Gear. A rule of thumb: write the second flow before you decide the first one is finished. If it can use the sheet as it is, the split is right. If it needs a new field to make sense, ask whether the rule is general or the field belongs to the first story.

Honest limit: neither GameSystem in this book has a second flow. Hard Country has The Long Carry and Dogwatch has Cold Berth. A second flow for Hard Country would be a different journey with the same bearers, say a winter siege or a harvest fair. It would pick from the same Scene Types where they fit and add its own where they do not. Trackers are declared once in the GameSystem, and each flow lists the Trackers and oracles it reaches, so the new flow would name only its own.

## What a flow cannot read

A flow is written on top of the rules, and it sees less of them than you would like. A step `condition` branches on Trackers and item counts. It does not read sheet fields. A Hard Country bearer's Wind is on the sheet, so no step can say "if Wind is 0, then...".

That is the engine's limit, and both presets live with it the same way. The harm rule is a sentence in a step tip: a bearer with no Wind marks a Wound, a crew member with no Nerve marks an Injury. The effects take the Wind or the Nerve, and the player marks the rest. When your rules need a hard branch on a sheet value, push the number into a Tracker, which a flow can read, or write the branch as a question for the player.

## The rail follows the order

Studio lists the sections of a GameSystem in the order this book teaches them: Overview, Flows, Scene Types, Trackers & tags, Oracles, Sheet, Checks, Gear, Library and Releases. Story first, then the rules. That is a path to follow, not a law. A rules-heavy game may start with the sheet.

The next pages take the rules in the order a Character meets them: the sheet, what it computes, the Checks, and what it carries.

## Try it

Take a game you would like to run solo. Write two lists, one headed Rules and one headed Story, and sort every idea you have into one of them. For each Rules item, ask whether a second story would use it unchanged.
