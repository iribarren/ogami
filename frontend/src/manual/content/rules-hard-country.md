# Hard Country Rules

A rules-light fantasy game about hard travel, where a body gives out before a story does. One sheet, for a bearer. The Long Carry is played on it, and the cards are printed separately.

## Making a bearer

- **Name and calling.** What you did before the carry: a ferryman, a nun, a deserter.
- **Traits.** Roll 2d6+3 for each of Back, Feet and Head.

| Trait | Used to                         |
| ----- | ------------------------------- |
| Back  | Carry, fight, endure            |
| Feet  | Move, dodge, sneak              |
| Head  | Notice, reason, hold your nerve |

- **Wind.** Roll d6+2. Wind is your breath and your nerve, and it is never above 8.
- **Pennies.** Roll 2d6 and multiply by 10.
- **Load.** Eight slots.

## Checks

Name the trait that fits what you try. Roll a d20.

| Roll                  | Result        |
| --------------------- | ------------- |
| 1                     | Clean success |
| At or under the trait | Success       |
| Over the trait        | Failure       |
| 20                    | Goes badly    |

The flow says what a failure or a bad result costs, and it usually names a Tracker or Wind.

## Harm

Harm takes Wind first. With no Wind left, each harm is a **Wound**: mark a box. Three Wounds put you down. Wounds do not heal.

Wind comes back only with a rest in Camp: roll d6+2, up to 8.

## Load

You carry eight slots. Most things take one slot. Heavy things take two. Lamp oil takes none.

| Kit            | Cost | Slots |
| -------------- | ---- | ----- |
| Rope           | 8    | 1     |
| Lantern        | 12   | 1     |
| Lamp oil       | 2    | 0     |
| Staff          | 3    | 1     |
| Blanket        | 6    | 1     |
| Bandages       | 4    | 1     |
| Waterskin      | 3    | 1     |
| Trail rations  | 2    | 1     |
| Carrying poles | 10   | 2     |

| Arms     | Cost | Slots |
| -------- | ---- | ----- |
| Knife    | 5    | 1     |
| Club     | 2    | 1     |
| Sling    | 4    | 1     |
| Pole axe | 20   | 2     |

Costs are in pennies. Bandages close a cut; they do not mend a Wound.
