# The Arc

The promise says what the story is about, and the arc says in what order it happens. It is a short list of **Phases**, each of them a stretch of play with one job to do.

## Phases

A Phase is a named part of the flow, such as the setup, the long middle or the ending. Give each one three things.

- A **name** you would say aloud, like "The Road", "Wake" or "Epilogue".
- A **description** of what the Phase is for and when it is done. "It is done when you have played all three scenes" makes a good last sentence, because a Phase without an end is a place you can get lost in.
- A **run**, which is either `once` or `loop`.

Most flows need four or five Phases, so a list of ten means that some of them are scenes.

## Once and loop

A `once` Phase is played through one time and is then over. Use it for the things that happen once in a story, such as making the characters, an arrival or an ending. Its scenes come in a fixed order, or as a short list that you work through.

A `loop` Phase repeats. In each round you play a scene, check whether the Phase is over, and then play the World turn, a short step in which a clock ticks and a table is rolled. Use it for the long middle of the story, where the same pattern comes round again and again and looks different each time.

A loop needs an exit, and the exit must not be the moment you get bored. The usual exit is a clock from your promise card, which you check at the end of every scene.

1. Read the clock.
2. If it is low, the loop goes on.
3. If it is in the middle, roll a die, and a high result ends the loop.
4. If it is full, the loop ends without a roll.

The roll in the middle is what makes the ending feel found rather than scheduled, because you know it can come soon without knowing which scene it will be.

## Acts

An **Act** is an optional label you put on a Phase to group it with its neighbours, such as "Act 2: The big job". It is only a label and not a level of the flow, so it changes nothing about how the Phases run. Use Acts in a long flow, when you want to say "we are in the second third". Neither of the two flows in this book uses them, because both are short.

## Session Zero

By convention the first Phase is **Session Zero**. It runs once, and in it you make everything the later Phases will need, such as the characters, the place, the people and the questions.

Ask of every later Phase what it will pick from, and make sure that Session Zero has made it. A loop that sends you to a compartment has to find compartments in the notebook, and a road that meets a stranger needs a name ready for the first one. When a Phase has nothing to draw on, you will make it up in the middle of play, and you will be a worse game master than the paper would have been.

Session Zero is also where you set down what you do not know, since a hunch you write now is something the story can prove or break later.

## On paper

Rule a page of the notebook with one row per Phase, and fill in these columns.

| Phase           | Run  | Done when                | What it needs              |
| --------------- | ---- | ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| The setup       | once | I have played its scenes | Nothing                    |
| The long middle | loop | The clock says so        | Whatever Session Zero made |
| The ending      | once | I have written it        | The clock and the counters |

Below the rows, draw the clock that closes the loop as a row of boxes, and mark the box where you start rolling. Then add a second line for each Phase, with the question that Phase answers.

Keep the page open while you play, so that when a scene ends you look here instead of trusting your memory.

:::in-ogami
In Studio, open the flow from **Flows** and use **Add phase** in the outline on the left. A Phase has a **Key**, a **Name**, an **Act** (leave it empty for none), a **Mode** (**Once** or **Loop**) and a **Description**. Play shows the description under the Phase, and again when the player ends it, so write it as the "when it is done" sentence. A Phase with no Scene Type cannot be saved yet; add its scenes from the Phase's selection rule, which the next chapters cover.

To end a loop, put a `condition` step in the Phase's **Scene closing**, with a band that has an `endPhase` effect (**Add effect**). The Long Carry and Cold Berth both do this. Session Zero is an ordinary Phase named "Session Zero", with the **Once** mode.
:::

## The Long Carry

| Phase           | Run  | What it does                                                |
| --------------- | ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Session Zero    | once | The bearers, the saint, the vow                             |
| The Road        | loop | A scene from the road deck each round, then the world turns |
| The Shrine Gate | once | The arrival                                                 |
| Epilogue        | once | One of three endings                                        |

**Session Zero** has three scenes, played in order, and each one makes something the Road will use, first the bearers and what they carry, then the saint and where the shrine stands, and last the vow. When it ends, the notebook holds two or three bearers, a place and three sentences.

**The Road** is the loop. Each round a scene from the road deck is played, and then Winter is read. Winter has ten boxes, and up to the fifth the Road goes on. From the sixth to the ninth a d6 is rolled, and a 5 or a 6 puts the gate in sight, which ends the Phase. At the tenth box the first snow ends it without a roll. The World turn adds one to Winter every round, so the clock sets a limit on how many rounds the Road can last.

**The Shrine Gate** is one scene, a conversation with the keeper. **Epilogue** reads Winter and Burden and gives one of three endings.

A scene has just ended with Winter at 6, and the player reads the clock.

> I read Winter, and it is at six. That is "Near", so I roll the d6 and get a 5. The gate comes into sight, and the Road ends.
>
> I write one line before I turn the page: "The litter is lighter than it was. I tell myself that is the cold."

Before that round, the World turn had rolled Road Risks, and the table gave _Smoke stands on the ridge where no one should be living._ That entry sent the next scene to a Ruin, so the table decided what the last stretch of the road looked like, while the Phase decided when the story ended.

## Cold Berth

| Phase        | Run  | What it does                                             |
| ------------ | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Session Zero | once | The crew, the ship, the Truths                           |
| Wake         | once | The empty berth, the custodian                           |
| The Ship     | loop | A job and a compartment each round, then the world turns |
| Reveal       | once | The Truths are opened                                    |
| Epilogue     | once | Who gets out                                             |

**Session Zero** has three scenes. The crew scene makes two or three crew cards, and the ship scene names the compartments (the Wake Bay, the Drive Spine, the Cold Hold, as many as you like) and the others on the manifest. These are the places and the people that the Ship Phase picks from later, which is why a Ship round never finds the ship empty. The third scene asks what you fear is aboard, who you think let it on and why you think you were woken. On paper you also write the real answers on a page and seal it, and Chapter 9 explains how.

**Wake** is two scenes that set the story moving.

**The Ship** is the loop. Threat has eight boxes, and up to the fourth the loop goes on. From the fifth to the seventh a d6 is rolled, and a 5 or a 6 brings the Reveal. At the eighth box the Reveal comes for certain.

**Reveal** is one scene in which the three Truths are opened and compared with your hunches. **Epilogue** reads Threat and Clues.

An Investigate scene has just ended with Threat at 6.

> Threat is six, which is "Rising". I roll the d6 and get a 3, so not yet.
>
> Now comes the World turn, and Threat climbs to seven. I roll Ship trouble and get _The air tastes of metal. The scrubbers are struggling._ Threat climbs again, to eight.

When the next scene ends, Threat will be at its full value and the Reveal will come without a roll. No entry in the table called for the Reveal, which followed from the ship being left to run.

## What to take away

- Name each Phase, say when it is done, and say whether it runs once or loops.
- End every loop with a clock, and put a die in the middle of the clock.
- Make everything a loop will pick from before the loop begins.
- Add Acts only when a flow is long enough to need them.
