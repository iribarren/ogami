# The Arc

The promise says what the story is about. The arc says what order it happens in. It is a short list of **Phases**, and each Phase is a stretch of play with one job.

## Phases

A Phase is a named part of the flow, such as the setup, the long middle or the ending. Give each one three things.

- A **name** you would say aloud: "The Road", "Wake", "Epilogue".
- A **description**: what the Phase is for, and when it is done. "It is done when you have played all three scenes" is a good last sentence. A Phase without an end is a place you can get lost in.
- A **run**: `once` or `loop`.

Most flows need four or five Phases. If you have ten, some of them are scenes.

## Once and loop

A `once` Phase is played through one time and then it is over. Use it for things that happen once: making characters, an arrival, an ending. Its scenes come in a fixed order or in a short list you work through.

A `loop` Phase repeats. Each round you play a scene, check whether the Phase is over, and then let the world turn: a short step where a clock ticks and a table is rolled. Use it for the long middle, where the story is a pattern repeated with a different face each time.

A loop needs an exit, and the exit must not be you getting bored. The usual one is a clock from your promise card, checked at the end of every scene:

1. Read the clock.
2. If it is low, the loop goes on.
3. If it is in the middle, roll a die. A high result ends the loop.
4. If it is full, the loop ends without a roll.

The roll in the middle is what makes the ending feel found and not scheduled. You know it can come soon. You do not know which scene it is.

## Acts

An **Act** is an optional label you put on a Phase to group it with its neighbours: "Act 2: The big job". It is only a label. It is not a level of the flow, and it changes nothing about how the Phases run. Use Acts in a long flow, when you want to say "we are in the second third". Neither of the two flows in this book uses them, because both are short.

## Session Zero

By convention the first Phase is **Session Zero**. It is where you make everything the later Phases will need: the characters, the place, the people, the questions. It runs once.

Ask of every later Phase what it will pick from, and make sure Session Zero has made it. A loop that sends you to a compartment must find compartments in the notebook. A road that meets a stranger must have a name for the first one. If a Phase has nothing to draw on, you will make it up at the table, and you will be a worse game master than the paper would have been.

Session Zero is also where you set down what you do not know. A hunch written now is something the story can prove or break later.

## On paper

Rule a page of the notebook into one row per Phase, and fill in these columns.

| Phase           | Run  | Done when                | What it needs              |
| --------------- | ---- | ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| The setup       | once | I have played its scenes | Nothing                    |
| The long middle | loop | The clock says so        | Whatever Session Zero made |
| The ending      | once | I have written it        | The clock and the counters |

Under the table draw the clock that closes the loop: a row of boxes, with the box where you start rolling marked. Add a second line for each Phase: the question it answers.

Keep the page open while you play. When a scene ends, you look here, not at your memory.

:::in-ogami
In Studio, open the flow from **Flows** and use **Add phase** in the outline on the left. A Phase has a **Key**, a **Name**, an **Act** (leave it empty for none), a **Mode** (**Once** or **Loop**) and a **Description**. Play shows the description under the Phase, and again when the player ends it, so write it as the "when it is done" sentence. A Phase with no Scene Type cannot be saved yet; add its scenes from the Phase's selection rule, which the next chapters cover.

To end a loop, put a `condition` step in the Phase's **Scene closing**, with a band that has an `endPhase` effect (**Add effect**). The Long Carry and Cold Berth both do this. Session Zero is an ordinary Phase named "Session Zero", with the **Once** mode.
:::

## The Long Carry

| Phase           | Run  | What it does                                                |
| --------------- | ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Session Zero    | once | The bearers, the saint, the vow                             |
| The Road        | loop | A scene from the road deck each round, then the world turns |
| The Shrine Gate | once | The arrival                                                 |
| Epilogue        | once | One of three endings                                        |

**Session Zero** has three scenes, in order. Each one makes something the Road will use: the bearers and what they carry, the saint and where the shrine stands, the vow. When it ends the notebook holds two or three bearers, a place and three sentences.

**The Road** is the loop. A scene from the road deck is played, and then Winter is read. Winter has ten boxes. Up to the fifth the Road goes on. From the sixth to the ninth, a d6 is rolled, and a 5 or a 6 puts the gate in sight, which ends the Phase. At the tenth box the first snow ends it without a roll. In between, the world turn adds one to Winter every round, so a long Road is a short one in disguise.

**The Shrine Gate** is one scene, a conversation with the keeper. **The Epilogue** reads Winter and Burden and gives one of three endings.

A played round. Winter stands at 6 when a scene ends.

> I read Winter: six. That is "Near", so I roll the d6. A 5. The gate comes into sight, and the Road ends.
>
> I write one line before I turn the page: "The litter is lighter than it was. I tell myself that is the cold."

Before that round the world turn rolled Road Risks, and the table gave _Smoke stands on the ridge where no one should be living._ It sent the next scene to a Ruin. The Phase decided when the story ended; the table decided what the last stretch looked like.

## Cold Berth

| Phase        | Run  | What it does                                             |
| ------------ | ---- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Session Zero | once | The crew, the ship, the Truths                           |
| Wake         | once | The empty berth, the custodian                           |
| The Ship     | loop | A job and a compartment each round, then the world turns |
| Reveal       | once | The Truths are opened                                    |
| Epilogue     | once | Who gets out                                             |

**Session Zero** has three scenes. The crew scene makes two or three crew cards. The ship scene names the compartments (the Wake Bay, the Drive Spine, the Cold Hold, as many as you like) and the others on the manifest. These are the places and the people the Ship Phase will pick from later, so a Ship round never finds the ship empty. The third scene asks what you fear is aboard, who you think let it on and why you think you were woken. On paper you also write the real answers on a page and seal it: Chapter 9 explains how.

**Wake** is two scenes that start the story in motion.

**The Ship** is the loop. Threat has eight boxes. Up to the fourth the loop goes on. From the fifth to the seventh, a d6 is rolled, and a 5 or a 6 brings the Reveal. At the eighth box the Reveal comes for certain.

**The Reveal** is one scene where the three Truths are opened and compared with your hunches. **The Epilogue** reads Threat and Clues.

A played round. Threat is 6 when an Investigate scene ends.

> Threat is six, which is "Rising". I roll the d6: a 3. Not yet.
>
> Now the world turn. Threat climbs to seven. I roll Ship trouble: _The air tastes of metal. The scrubbers are struggling._ Threat climbs again, to eight.

The next scene will end with Threat at its full value, and the Reveal comes without a roll. Nothing in the table forced the Reveal. It followed from the ship being left to run.

## What to take away

- Name each Phase, say when it is done, and say whether it runs once or loops.
- End every loop with a clock, and put a die in the middle of the clock.
- Make everything a loop will pick from before the loop begins.
- Add Acts only when a flow is long enough to need them.
