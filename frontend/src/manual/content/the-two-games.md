# The two games

The book is built around two games, with one flow for each. A **GameSystem** is a game in the sense you probably know from other roleplaying games, the rules you learn once and then bring to every story, such as its dice, its checks and its character sheet. A **NarrativeFlow**, which we usually call a flow, is a guided way of playing one story inside that GameSystem. It sets out where the story starts and how it can end, and it leaves the way between them to the dice, the cards and your own answers. That is why the same flow turns out different each time you play it, and different again when someone else does. One GameSystem can hold many flows, although here each holds only one.

As the book goes on, we build a flow one piece at a time, a chapter for each piece, and show every piece in both of these games so that you can build your own beside them.

## The Long Carry, on Hard Country

_Will you get the saint to the shrine before first snow, and what will it cost?_

Hard Country is a rules-light fantasy game about hard travel, where a body gives out before a story does, and in The Long Carry you play a small group of bearers carrying a saint's body across hard country. A character has three traits, Back, Feet and Head, and a pool of Wind that harm wears down first. The Long Carry is played over three to four sittings, which the book calls sessions.

The story has four parts. It opens with **Session Zero**, where you set out who the bearers are, who the saint was and the vow that sent you. Most of the play happens on **The Road**, which comes round again and again. Each time, a shuffled deck of scene cards decides what you meet next, whether a camp, a ford, a stranger, a ruin or an omen, and the card for the last scene is hidden near the bottom of the deck. After each scene the world takes a turn of its own, the World turn. It moves the **Winter** clock on as the season closes in, and rolls a die on a short list of road risks. **The Shrine Gate** is the arrival, and **Epilogue** has three endings, chosen by how far Winter has come and by the **Burden** you carry.

The saint's story has a Truth, a secret at its heart, and here the Truth is emergent, so you write what the saint's sin was as the omens pile up.

## Cold Berth, on Dogwatch

_What is aboard, who let it on, and why were we woken?_

Dogwatch is a minimal science-fiction game, and in Cold Berth you play crew members woken early from cold sleep on a ship that should be empty. A character is a few numbers, and when an action could go either way you make a check, which is a single die. Cold Berth is played over two to three sessions.

This story also opens with **Session Zero**, which gives each of you a crew card and seals three Truths in the notebook. **Wake** follows, with the empty berth and the custodian's refusal to explain. The middle of the story is **The Ship**, which repeats as the Road does. Each time you choose what to do (investigate, repair, confront or hide) and then which compartment of the ship to go to, and after every scene the World turn raises the **Threat** clock and picks a crew member to go missing. **Reveal** opens the sealed Truths, and **Epilogue** says who gets out, going by the **Clues** you found and the Threat that remains.

Here the Truths are sealed instead, which means they are written before play and opened at the end.

## Why two

The two flows make opposite choices. One lets the Truth appear as you play and the other hides it from the start. One shuffles its scenes into a deck, whereas the other lets you pick. Because each chapter shows its idea with both flows, you can decide which choice suits the story you want.
