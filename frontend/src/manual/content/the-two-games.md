# The two games

The book is built around two games, with one flow for each. A **GameSystem** holds a game's rules (its dice, its checks, its character sheet), while a **NarrativeFlow** is one story inside that GameSystem. One GameSystem can hold many flows, although here each holds only one.

## The Long Carry, on Hard Country

_Will you get the saint to the shrine before first snow, and what will it cost?_

Hard Country is a rules-light fantasy game about hard travel, where a body gives out before a story does, and in The Long Carry you play a small group of bearers carrying a saint's body across hard country. A character has three traits, Back, Feet and Head, and a pool of Wind that harm wears down first. The Long Carry runs for three to four sessions.

- **Session Zero** sets the bearers, the saint and the vow that sent you.
- **The Road** repeats. A shuffled deck of scenes decides what you meet next, whether a camp, a ford, a stranger, a ruin or an omen, and the last scene is hidden near the bottom of the deck. Each round, the World turn moves the **Winter** clock and rolls a small table of road risks.
- **The Shrine Gate** is the arrival.
- **Epilogue** has three endings, chosen by Winter and by the **Burden** you carry.

The Truth of the story is emergent, so you write what the saint's sin was as the omens pile up.

## Cold Berth, on Dogwatch

_What is aboard, who let it on, and why were we woken?_

Dogwatch is a minimal science-fiction game, and in Cold Berth you play crew members woken early from cold sleep on a ship that should be empty. A character is a few numbers, and a check is a single die. Cold Berth runs for two to three sessions.

- **Session Zero** gives each of you a crew card and seals three Truths in the notebook.
- **Wake** is the empty berth and the custodian's refusal to explain.
- **The Ship** repeats. You choose what to do (investigate, repair, confront or hide) and then which compartment to go to. Each round, the World turn raises the **Threat** clock and picks a crew member who is missing.
- **Reveal** opens the sealed Truths.
- **Epilogue** says who gets out, going by the **Clues** you found and the Threat that remains.

Here the Truth is sealed instead, which means it is written before play and opened at the end.

## Why two

The two flows make opposite choices. One lets the Truth appear as you play and the other hides it from the start. One shuffles its scenes into a deck, whereas the other lets you pick. Because each chapter shows its idea with both flows, you can decide which choice suits the story you want.
