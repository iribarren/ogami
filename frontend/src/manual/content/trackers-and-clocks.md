# Trackers and Clocks at the Table

Part I explained why a flow keeps numbers and what each kind is for. This chapter is about the paper: how to draw them, how to keep them honest in the middle of a scene, and what you do when one runs out.

A **Tracker** is only useful if you look at it. The best Tracker is the one you cannot help reading every time you turn the page.

## Drawing them

**A clock** is a row of boxes. Draw one box per segment and put a number under every second one, so you can find your place at a glance. Mark the box where the dice start, with a small flag or a different colour. When you reach it, the loop changes character, and you want to see that coming.

**A counter** is a line with the numbers on it and a ring around the current one. Write the names of its stretches under the line. Burden counts from 0 to 6, and its stretches are Light, Heavy, Crushing and Breaking. A name tells you more than a number does, so read the name first.

Keep every Tracker for one flow on the same page, at the top, where you will see it before anything else. Add the hint, the one line of what it measures, next to each.

## What fills them

Write down, before play, what moves each Tracker. There are three sources.

- **The world turn.** The clock moves by one, every time, whatever you did.
- **Tables.** A line says **(Winter +1)** and you mark it.
- **Checks and steps.** A failed check costs a box. A step says to add one.

If something moves a Tracker and you cannot say which of these it was, you have made a rule up. That is allowed, but write it down so you do it the same way next time.

Mark a Tracker the moment the cause happens, not at the end of the scene. A box marked late is a box forgotten.

## When one fills

A full clock is a promise the game made at the start. Decide what it is before you begin, and write it on the page.

- **It ends a Phase.** A road ends when the snow comes; a ship stops waking you when the Threat clock is full.
- **It forces a scene.** A full clock sends you to the scene you were avoiding.
- **It closes an ending.** The Tracker's final value picks one of the endings. The Epilogue of each flow reads two Trackers and chooses from them.

A counter does not fill; it moves. Decide what its extremes mean. Burden at its top is a bearer who cannot walk. Clues at its top is a whole picture, and the Reveal takes it from there.

When a Tracker has reached its top, leave it there. Do not carry the overflow into another Tracker unless a rule says so.

### Going down

Some Trackers can fall. A Repair that is done takes a box off Threat, in Cold Berth, and that is the only way the clock falls. Make a falling Tracker rare and make it pay for a scene. If it falls often, the clock stops being a clock.

## Keeping them honest

A Tracker you stop trusting stops working. Three habits keep it honest.

- **Say the number out loud when it moves.** "Winter, five." It takes a second and catches the box you forgot.
- **Never move one to be kind to yourself.** If the clock says the road is over, the road is over. A game that rescues you teaches you nothing.
- **Look back at the end of a session.** Count the marks against the journal. A mismatch is a scene you did not write down.

## On paper

Draw the page once for the whole flow and copy it to a fresh sheet for each session, with the marks carried over. At the end of every session, write the date, the state of each Tracker and one line of what changed.

Two cards beside the page make the rest quick: the **World turn card**, a checklist you read after every scene, and the **closing card**, which says what each clock does when it fills.

:::in-ogami
In Studio, a Tracker is declared on the **Trackers & tags** page with **New tracker**: a **Key**, a **Name**, a **Hint** and a **Kind**, **Counter** or **Clock**. A clock has **Segments**. A counter has **Min**, **Max**, **Initial** and optional levels. A step's **Add effect** can add to a Tracker. A **condition** step reads a Tracker and sends the flow down a band, and a band can carry an `endPhase` effect.

In Play, the **Trackers** panel draws each Tracker as filled and empty boxes, with its level label and its hint. You can move one by hand with the **Decrease** and **Increase** buttons, or type a number and press **Set**. What happens at the last box is the flow's business, through a condition step, and not the panel's.
:::

## The Long Carry

Winter is a clock of ten. Burden is a counter from 0 to 6 with four stretches. The Long Carry marks Winter every turn, and Burden whenever the bearers take on weight: a bad foot, a favour, a failed ford.

On paper this page is simple. A row of ten boxes for Winter, with a flag on the sixth. A line from 0 to 6 for Burden, with Light, Heavy, Crushing and Breaking under it.

A played mark. The ford at the end of a hard day, a Back check, and the player rolls a 20.

> Going badly. The ford takes d6 Wind, and Burden goes up by one, from 1 to 2. That is the top of Heavy. I draw the ring around the 2 and write the stretch beside it.

One more, and the bearers are Crushing. The name changes before the number gets frightening.

## Cold Berth

Threat is a clock of eight. Clues is a counter from 0 to 8 with the stretches Blind, Hints, A shape and The whole picture.

Threat fills for good: when it is full, the closing check ends the Ship Phase. It can also fall, but only through a Repair. Clues only climbs, and it is read at the end, in the Epilogue, with Threat, to choose how the crew gets out.

A played mark. A Repair on a failed scrubber. The roll is a d8 with Difficulty 3, and the result is a 6: done.

> Threat comes down by one, from five to four. That brings it to the edge of the quiet stretch. I cross out the old mark in the fifth box and write what has changed: the scrubbers hold.

A fall cost a scene, and bought a scene's worth of quiet.

## Try it

Draw the Trackers of your flow on one page. For each, write what moves it, what it does when it reaches the top, and the name of the stretch that makes you nervous.
