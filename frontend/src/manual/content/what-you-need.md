# What you need

A solo game needs very little. Gather these before you first sit down to play.

## Always

::image{file="house-kit.webp" style="house" size="half" alt="A notebook, a pencil, a set of dice, a deck of cards and index cards laid out on a table, seen from above." subject="A notebook, a pencil, a set of dice, a small deck of cards and a few index cards laid out on a plain table, seen from above, all cards blank."}

- **A pencil and a notebook.** The notebook is the story's memory. It is where you keep what your characters did, how far you have come, the people you met on the way and the clocks you started. Any paper works, and so does a text file on a computer if you would rather type.
- **Polyhedral dice, d4 to d20.** One each of d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 and d20. A second d6 and a second d20 are useful. A dice roller on your phone is an acceptable substitute.
- **A deck of playing cards.** A standard 52-card deck. The book uses it as an oracle, a way of letting chance answer a question, and as a shuffled stack of scenes.

## Helpful

- About fifty index cards, so that each scene, each person and each place of your flow can have a card of its own, which you can draw, stack and shuffle.
- A few coloured pens, to tell your clocks and threads apart.
- Paper clips or a small box, to keep the face-up and face-down piles apart.
- A timer, if you like to play for a set length of time.

## How long

A first session, which is one sitting at the table, takes about an hour, and the two example flows run for two to four sessions each. Stop at a natural pause, such as the end of a scene or of a stretch of the story, rather than when a set time runs out.

## Where you play

Play somewhere quiet that you can settle into, with room on the table to spread out a few cards. The game needs your attention, and setting the mood before you begin, perhaps with some music that suits the story, helps you give it, because a mood you have chosen keeps you inside the story when your mind would otherwise wander. You will not need a game master, since you hold the pencil and the book gives the questions.
